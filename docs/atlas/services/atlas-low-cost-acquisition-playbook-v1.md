# Atlas Low-Cost Acquisition Playbook v1

> Internal product/operations spec for low-budget, high-ROI marketing campaigns in CDMX. This complements `atlas-growth-audit-sprint-v1.md` by defining what Atlas should recommend and execute after the audit.

## 1. Strategic thesis

Atlas should not sell generic social media management. The default product should be a low-cost customer acquisition and retention system centered on:

1. WhatsApp-first conversion.
2. Meta click-to-WhatsApp and retargeting.
3. Google Business Profile and local SEO.
4. Google Search for high-intent local demand.
5. Email/CRM reactivation when a client has any usable database.
6. Referrals and partnerships.
7. TikTok/marketplaces only when the business model fits.

The operating principle:

> Manual/direct acquisition before broad paid media. Conversation before content volume. Offer before branding. Tracking before scale.

## 2. Productized offers

### 2.1 Conversión Local IA

Anchor offer for most CDMX SMBs.

Best for:

- Clinics.
- Beauty/aesthetic services.
- Dental.
- Local services.
- Home services.
- Fitness studios.
- Restaurants/bars with reservations/events.
- B2B local services requiring quote/cita.

Core system:

- WhatsApp funnel.
- Meta click-to-WhatsApp or Google Search.
- Simple landing page when needed.
- CRM/lead tracker.
- Response scripts.
- Follow-up sequence.
- Dashboard.

Commercial model for early friends/family pilots:

- Free audit.
- Optional setup/service asset fee.
- Commission on qualified leads, booked appointments, or profit from closed sales.

Longer-term suggested pricing:

- Setup: MXN 6,000–12,000.
- Monthly: MXN 10,000–18,000.
- Success fee: per qualified appointment or percentage of incremental profit/revenue.

### 2.2 Retención y Reactivación

Best for businesses with a customer or lead database.

Core system:

- Welcome flow.
- Lead reactivation.
- No-show recovery.
- Post-sale follow-up.
- Review request.
- Referral loop.
- Tactical newsletter or campaign emails.

Commercial model:

- Optional setup.
- Commission on attributed reactivated revenue.
- Lower media spend than acquisition campaigns.

### 2.3 Marketplace Growth

Selective product for businesses with a catalog.

Best for:

- Retail.
- Packaged goods.
- Homeware.
- Beauty/accessories.
- Standardized products.

Core system:

- Listing optimization.
- Photos/titles/descriptions.
- Review generation.
- Bundles/promos.
- Internal marketplace ads if economics fit.
- External remarketing only after listings convert.

## 3. Channel priority

### Priority 1 — highest ROI / lowest waste

1. Warm intros.
2. Direct WhatsApp/Instagram/email outreach.
3. WhatsApp conversion flows.
4. Google Business Profile and reviews.
5. Referral/reactivation loops.

### Priority 2 — scalable once offer is clear

1. Meta click-to-WhatsApp.
2. Google Search local high-intent keywords.
3. Landing page + WhatsApp flow.
4. Email/CRM automation.

### Priority 3 — selective

1. Instagram/Facebook organic and retargeting.
2. TikTok organic/Promote.
3. Marketplaces.
4. Broad awareness/content campaigns.

## 4. Campaign blueprint

Every Atlas campaign should have:

- Target segment.
- Pain/desire.
- Specific offer.
- One CTA.
- Channel choice.
- Trust asset.
- Follow-up sequence.
- Tracking and attribution.
- Success fee logic.
- Kill/scale criteria.

Campaign hypothesis format:

> If we show `[segment]` the offer `[offer]` through `[channel]`, then `[conversion action]` will happen because `[reason]`.

## 5. Minimum viable campaign

Default first test:

- 1 target segment.
- 1 offer.
- 1 trust asset.
- 1 primary channel.
- 50–100 prospects or MXN 3,000–6,000 test media budget.
- 2–3 follow-ups.
- 7–14 day readout.
- Manual tracking.

Do not scale until there is signal.

Signal means:

- Replies.
- Qualified conversations.
- Quote requests.
- Booked appointments.
- Closed sales.

## 6. Core playbooks

### 6.1 WhatsApp + Meta click-to-WhatsApp

Flow:

1. Ad/post/referral.
2. WhatsApp chat.
3. Qualification.
4. Appointment or quote.
5. Human follow-up within SLA.
6. Close.
7. Post-sale review/referral.

Best for:

- Services requiring quote/cita.
- Local businesses with fast response capacity.
- Offers that can be explained quickly.

Minimum stack:

- Meta Ads Manager.
- WhatsApp Business App or Platform.
- CRM/tracking sheet.
- Calendar if appointments matter.

KPIs:

- Cost per conversation.
- Response rate.
- Qualified lead rate.
- Appointment rate.
- Show rate.
- Close rate.
- Profit and commission.

### 6.2 Google Business Profile + local SEO

Flow:

1. Optimize categories/services/description.
2. Add current photos.
3. Add products/services.
4. Improve calls/WhatsApp/site link.
5. Request reviews.
6. Respond to reviews/Q&A.
7. Publish local posts/offers.

Best for:

- Restaurants/bars.
- Cleaning/home services.
- Clinics/salons/fitness.
- Any local-intent business.

KPIs:

- Calls.
- Website clicks.
- Direction requests.
- WhatsApp inquiries.
- Review velocity.
- Local ranking/query visibility.

### 6.3 Email/CRM reactivation

Flow:

1. Clean/import list.
2. Segment.
3. Welcome/reactivation sequence.
4. Offer CTA.
5. Follow-up based on click/reply.
6. Track revenue/appointments.

Best for:

- Businesses with existing leads/customers.
- Dormant projects.
- Repeat-purchase businesses.

Measure:

- Clicks.
- Replies.
- Appointments.
- Revenue per send.
- Unsubscribes/spam.

Do not over-optimize open rate because privacy features distort it.

### 6.4 Referral and alliance loop

Flow:

1. Identify happy customers/partners.
2. Create simple incentive.
3. Ask at the right moment.
4. Track referrals.
5. Pay/credit reliably.

Best for:

- Trust-heavy services.
- Premium/local communities.
- Friends/family networks.

KPIs:

- Referral requests sent.
- Referrals received.
- Conversion rate.
- CAC vs paid channels.

### 6.5 Google Search local

Flow:

1. Exact/phrase high-intent keywords.
2. Local modifier.
3. Landing page or WhatsApp CTA.
4. Call/lead tracking.
5. Weekly search-term cleanup.

Best for:

- High-intent services.
- Problems people already search for.

Use only when:

- Offer and landing/conversation flow are ready.
- Tracking is in place.
- Economics support CPC.

## 7. Decision rules

### Launch criteria

A campaign should not launch unless:

- Segment is specific.
- Offer is concrete.
- CTA is singular.
- Tracking exists.
- Commission/revenue attribution is defined.
- Client effort is low or explicitly scoped.
- Trust asset exists.
- Follow-up is written.

### Kill criteria

Cut/rework if:

- CTR/reply is weak after enough impressions/messages.
- Conversations are unqualified.
- Qualified leads do not move to quote/cita.
- Profit cannot support acquisition cost.
- Attribution is disputed or impossible.

### Scale criteria

Scale only if:

- Leads are qualified.
- Sales or strong buying intent exists.
- Attribution is clear.
- Fulfillment capacity exists.
- CAC/payback is acceptable.

## 8. Campaign readiness scorecard

Score each 1–5.

1. Segment clarity.
2. Offer strength.
3. Economics.
4. Channel fit.
5. Attribution.
6. Client effort required.
7. Speed to signal.
8. Trust assets.
9. Follow-up system.
10. Scale path.

Rule:

- Minimum average: 4/5.
- No category below 3.
- No spend beyond tiny tests with any attribution/economics failure.

## 9. Compliance and risk

Atlas must treat WhatsApp/email/outbound as permission-sensitive channels.

Minimum requirements:

- Track source of lead.
- Track opt-in when collecting personal data.
- Maintain suppression/exclusion list.
- Provide clear business identity in messages.
- Respect no-contact requests.
- Avoid unsupported claims, especially in regulated categories.
- Keep privacy notice/terms ready when collecting forms/leads.
- Avoid spammy WhatsApp template usage and high-frequency blasts.

## 10. Three-month rollout

### Month 1 — foundations

- Define anchor offer.
- Create templates/scripts.
- Set up tracking/dashboard.
- Produce landing/WhatsApp model.
- Run Legacy as internal pilot.

### Month 2 — validation

- Run 3–5 small experiments.
- Capture internal benchmarks.
- Produce 2–3 mini case studies.
- Refine pricing/commission terms.

### Month 3 — selective scale

- Double down on campaigns with signal.
- Add referral loop.
- Create SOPs.
- Formalize service packages and upsells.
